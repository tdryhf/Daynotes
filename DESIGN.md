## Overview

Daybook is a working family tool. Its first screen should make the daily habit check-in obvious, while the workbook's month-by-month record remains easy to find. The surface feels tactile and warm, with restrained color reserved for actions, progress, and status.

## Colors

Cream paper and deep ink carry most of the interface. Pear yellow marks the active view and main action. Mint shows completed progress. Coral and blue distinguish family tools when they need a quick visual cue. Keep text contrast strong and do not use color as the only state indicator.

## Typography

Use the rounded Chillax face for both headings and body text. Headings are compact and confident; everyday copy stays at a comfortable reading size. Use tabular numerals for dates, goals, and check-in totals. Avoid tiny decorative labels.

## Layout

On wide screens, keep a persistent family navigation rail beside the daily ledger and its supporting cards. On phones, collapse that rail to a labeled bottom dock and put secondary navigation behind the menu. At 320px, preserve a readable gutter and scroll the habit month grid inside its own panel.

## Elevation & Depth

Use a light paper grain over warm surfaces. Cards have a fine printed edge and a short, soft shadow. Primary controls use a small pressed-paper edge; hover motion is brief and optional. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Shapes

Cards use generous rounded corners, controls have softer stock-like corners, and chips are fully rounded. Keep borders faint and tactile. Use clear focus rings and mobile touch targets.

## Components

- **Primary button:** pear-yellow fill, dark ink, stock corners, short raised edge.
- **Quiet button:** transparent or paper fill with a clear hover/focus state.
- **Paper card:** off-white surface, subtle grain, rounded edge, low shadow.
- **Habit check:** a large outlined square that turns mint when marked done.
- **Habit calendar:** a horizontally scrollable date matrix with a pinned habit-name column.
- **Progress graph:** a seven-day completion line with readable day labels and a matching accessible text summary.
- **Goal bars:** short progress tracks for each habit's current weekly or monthly target.
- **Family navigation:** labeled rail on desktop; labeled, touch-sized dock on phone.
- **Form fields:** paper fill, visible border, clear label and focus outline.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Keep the Today check-in as the first, useful surface.
- Let check marks, totals, and dates read clearly in a quick glance.
- Keep sample content clearly marked and removable.
- State plainly that an open shared daybook can be viewed and changed by anyone with its link.
- Avoid generic gradient hero art, decorative tiny labels, and motion that slows down a check-in.
